---
title: "On Toby Ord’s “Broad Timelines”"
date: 2026-09-29
description: "Why overshooting and undershooting AI timelines shouldn’t be weighted equally, and why worrying about looking discredited shouldn’t slow down safety work."
category: ai-safety
---

I agree somewhat with the broad conclusion that Toby Ord makes in the post [“Broad Timelines”](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HCR2AE9it279ggiZT/broad-timelines) - that long-term projects that will take 5-10 years to do may still be worth pursuing despite the rapid advancement of AI. However, there are two claims I believe to be untrue and counter to humanity’s best interests. For one, Toby presents probability distributions on future AI timelines, with little regard to the consequences of overshooting and undershooting the timeline. In regards to AI safety, if we overprepare for short timelines, we end up understanding alignment and interpretability, so that we are extremely prepared for an AI takeoff years down the line, whatever that may look like. However, in the alternative case where transformative AI comes early and we are underprepared for it, catastrophic events take place as a result. These two outcomes should by no means be equally weighted. Toby touches upon this in his post: “I strongly recommend governments, civil society, and academics do more to hedge against transformative AI coming early.” However, it does not sound like he grasps just how different these two outcomes are as he goes on to state “... the communities of professionals already working on helping the AI transition go well, I think they are already hedging strongly against early transformative AI.” This is blatantly untrue - despite the insane progress and many red flags that have come up (e.g. the [Hugging Face incident](/writing/its-all-marketing/)), resources dedicated to safety and alignment research remain scarce compared to AI research as a whole.

Another claim he makes that I disagree with is “And if we haven’t played our cards right, those of us working on avoiding catastrophic risks from AI may have also lost a lot of power, with our ideas about AI risk being seen as discredited since so many years have passed without the truly transformative effects we were talking about.” I do not believe this is a concern that should be weighted heavily. For one, the research that goes into preparing for a transformative takeoff early on will be relevant, no matter what the timeline is. Misaligned AI is no less dangerous if it happens in 1 year or happens in 10, and the resources put into avoiding catastrophic risk will never go to waste (unless transformative AI is impossible, which is widely rejected at this point). The general public may become disgruntled with hearing about these catastrophic risks if they do not end up happening, but if disaster does not strike when transformative AI happens, it will be precisely because we took great measures to prevent it.
